# Product Introduction Quality Gates

## Product Truth

- Product name, category, audience, and capabilities match supplied evidence.
- Metrics, commands, integrations, customers, and comparisons are verified or marked `Evidence needed`.
- Source-product branding and wording are not copied.

## Story

- The opening names the product or category literally.
- The audience understands the problem before internal architecture appears.
- Each slide answers one question and has one memorable takeaway.
- Differentiation follows a fair explanation of alternatives.
- Cases show actions and artifacts, not only claims.
- The final slide gives one clear next action.

## Composition

- Text fits within the slide at the target viewport.
- Heading scale matches the amount of content.
- Cards represent real peer concepts or comparison items.
- Diagrams, tables, code, and product states remain inspectable.
- Semantic colors keep the same meaning throughout the deck.

## Motion

- Every reveal has a communication purpose.
- Existing content does not jump when new content appears.
- The complete state is understandable without animation.
- Reduced-motion and PDF-export states are defined.
- Auto-play is not required for comprehension.

## Implementation

- Slidev, Vue, theme, and icon dependencies are explicit.
- Direct Vercel routes refresh successfully.
- Code blocks and commands are accurate.
- External CTAs are valid.
- Images have stable dimensions and suitable alternatives.
- Console, build, export, and navigation checks pass.

## Public Reading

- The fixed presentation canvas is not presented as responsive mobile design.
- A responsive reading route, overview, transcript, or PDF is supplied when public mobile consumption matters.

## Final Acceptance

Another engineer must be able to implement the deck without deciding the chapter order, slide responsibilities, visual roles, reveal sequence, implementation stack, or deployment behavior.
